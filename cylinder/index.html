Cylinder

A cylinder is a solid with two equal circular ends joined by a curved surface. The distance between the ends is the height. A right circular cylinder, the usual kind, has its ends directly above each other.

Formulas

  • Volume = πr2h
  • Curved surface area = 2πrh
  • Total surface area = 2πr(r + h)

r = radius and h = height.

Open the calculator

Properties

  • 2 flat circular faces and 1 curved face.
  • 2 curved edges and no corners.
  • Every slice parallel to the ends is a circle of the same radius.
  • Unrolled, the curved surface is a rectangle.

Why the formulas work

Volume is base area times height, as for a box: the circular base has area πr² and it is stacked to height h. For the curved surface, picture the label of a tin. Peeled off and laid flat it is a rectangle, as wide as the circumference 2πr and as tall as the tin, so its area is 2πrh. Add the two circular ends for the total surface.

Worked example

Take a cylinder with radius 7 cm and height 10 cm.

  1. Volume. Volume = πr2h. Here: π × 7 × 7 × 10 = 1,539.38 cm³ (with π = 22/7: 1,540 cm³).
  2. Curved surface area. Curved surface area = 2πrh. Here: 2 × π × 7 × 10 = 439.82 cm² (with π = 22/7: 440 cm²).
  3. Total surface area. Total surface area = 2πr(r + h). Here: 2 × π × 7 × (7 + 10) = 747.7 cm² (with π = 22/7: 748 cm²).

Finding a missing measurement

Height = V ÷ πr². A cylinder of volume 1540 cm³ and radius 7 cm has a height of 1540 ÷ 154 = 10 cm, taking π = 22/7. From the curved surface, h = CSA ÷ 2πr: 440 cm² and r = 7 cm give 440 ÷ 44 = 10 cm.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Using the diameter as the radius.
  • For an open tank or a glass, add only one circular end, not two.
  • For a pipe open at both ends, the surface is only the curved part.

Where you see it

Tins, gas cylinders, water pipes, pillars, batteries, glasses, drums and road rollers are cylinders.
