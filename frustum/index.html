Frustum of a cone

A frustum of a cone is what remains when the top of a cone is cut off by a slice parallel to the base. It has two circular ends of different sizes. A bucket is the everyday example.

Formulas

  • Slant height l = √(h2 + (R − r)2)
  • Volume = ⅓ πh(R2 + Rr + r2)
  • Curved surface area = π(R + r)l
  • Total surface area = π(R + r)l + π(R2 + r2)

R = larger radius, r = smaller radius and h = height.

Open the calculator

Properties

  • 2 flat circular faces of different radii and 1 curved face.
  • The slant height, the height and the difference of the radii form a right-angled triangle.
  • When the two radii are equal, the formulas become those of a cylinder. When the smaller radius is 0, they become those of a cone.

Why the formulas work

A frustum is a big cone with a small cone removed from its top. The two cones are similar, and subtracting their volumes and tidying the algebra gives ⅓πh(R² + Rr + r²). The curved surface is found the same way, by subtracting the curved surfaces of the two cones, and comes to π(R + r)l.

Worked example

Take a frustum of a cone with larger radius 14 cm, smaller radius 7 cm and height 24 cm.

  1. Slant height. Slant height l = √(h2 + (R − r)2). Here: √(24² + (14 − 7)²) = 25 cm.
  2. Volume. Volume = ⅓ πh(R2 + Rr + r2). Here: ⅓ × π × 24 × (14² + 14 × 7 + 7²) = 8,620.53 cm³ (with π = 22/7: 8,624 cm³).
  3. Curved surface area. Curved surface area = π(R + r)l. Here: π × (14 + 7) × 25 = 1,649.34 cm² (with π = 22/7: 1,650 cm²).
  4. Total surface area. Total surface area = π(R + r)l + π(R2 + r2). Here: 1,649.34 + π × (14² + 7²) = 2,419.03 cm² (with π = 22/7: 2,420 cm²).

Finding a missing measurement

If the slant height is given and not the height, h = √(l² − (R − r)²): radii of 14 cm and 7 cm with a slant height of 25 cm give h = √(625 − 49) = 24 cm. For a bucket, the capacity in litres is the volume in cm³ divided by 1000.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Using the vertical height in the curved surface formula. It needs the slant height.
  • For an open bucket, add only the base circle, not both.
  • Averaging the radii and using the cylinder formula gives a wrong volume.

Where you see it

Buckets, drinking glasses, flower pots, lampshades and funnels (the wide part) are frustums.
