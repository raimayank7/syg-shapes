Hemisphere

A hemisphere is half of a sphere, cut by a flat slice through the centre. It has a curved surface and one flat circular face.

Formulas

  • Volume = (2 ⁄ 3) πr3
  • Curved surface area = 2πr2
  • Total surface area = 3πr2

r = radius.

Open the calculator

Properties

  • 1 curved face and 1 flat circular face.
  • 1 curved edge and no corners.
  • Its volume is half that of the sphere.
  • Its curved surface is half the surface of the sphere.

Why the formulas work

Half a sphere has half the volume, (2/3)πr³, and half the curved surface, 2πr². But cutting the sphere creates a new flat face, a circle of area πr². A solid hemisphere therefore has a total surface of 2πr² + πr² = 3πr². A hollow bowl has only the curved part.

Worked example

Take a hemisphere with radius 7 cm.

  1. Volume. Volume = (2 ⁄ 3) πr3. Here: (2 ⁄ 3) × π × 7 × 7 × 7 = 718.38 cm³ (with π = 22/7: 718.67 cm³).
  2. Curved surface area. Curved surface area = 2πr2. Here: 2 × π × 7 × 7 = 307.88 cm² (with π = 22/7: 308 cm²).
  3. Total surface area. Total surface area = 3πr2. Here: 3 × π × 7 × 7 = 461.81 cm² (with π = 22/7: 462 cm²).

Finding a missing measurement

From the total surface area of a solid hemisphere, r = √(T ÷ 3π): 462 cm² gives r² = 49, so r = 7 cm, taking π = 22/7. From the volume, r³ = 3V ÷ 2π: 19,404 cm³ gives r³ = 9261, so r = 21 cm.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Giving 2πr² when the question asks for the total surface of a solid hemisphere.
  • Adding the flat face for an open bowl, where it does not exist.

Where you see it

A bowl, a dome on a building, half a coconut shell, half a lemon and an igloo are hemispheres.
