Isosceles triangle

An isosceles triangle has two equal sides. The third side is called the base, and the two angles at the base are equal.

Formulas

  • Height = √(a2 − b2 ⁄ 4)
  • Area = ½ × b × height
  • Perimeter = 2a + b

a = equal side and b = base.

Open the calculator

Properties

  • Two sides are equal, and the angles opposite them are equal.
  • It has one line of symmetry, through the top corner and the middle of the base.
  • The height to the base cuts the base in half.
  • An equilateral triangle is a special isosceles triangle.

Why the formulas work

The height from the top corner meets the base at its middle point. That makes a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse a and one leg b/2, so the height is the root of a² minus b²/4. With the height known, the area is half of base times height.

Worked example

Take a isosceles triangle with equal side 13 cm and base 10 cm.

  1. Height. Height = √(a2 − b2 ⁄ 4). Here: √(13² − 10² ⁄ 4) = 12 cm.
  2. Area. Area = ½ × b × height. Here: ½ × 10 × 12 = 60 cm².
  3. Perimeter. Perimeter = 2a + b. Here: 2 × 13 + 10 = 36 cm.

Finding a missing measurement

If the base and the height are known, the equal side is √(h² + b² ⁄ 4): a base of 10 cm and a height of 12 cm give √(144 + 25) = 13 cm. From the perimeter, the base is the perimeter minus twice the equal side.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Taking the equal side as the height.
  • Forgetting to halve the base before using Pythagoras.
  • The two equal sides must together be longer than the base, or no triangle can be made.

Where you see it

A clothes hanger, the gable end of a house, a slice of pizza (nearly) and many roof trusses are isosceles triangles.
