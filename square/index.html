Square

A square is a flat shape with four equal sides and four right angles. It is the simplest shape to measure, and area itself is defined by it: one square centimetre is the area of a square of side 1 cm.

Formulas

  • Area = a2
  • Perimeter = 4a
  • Diagonal = a√2

a = side.

Open the calculator

Properties

  • All four sides are equal.
  • Every angle is 90°.
  • The two diagonals are equal, cut each other in half and cross at right angles.
  • It has four lines of symmetry.
  • A square is a special rectangle and also a special rhombus.

Why the formulas work

Lay unit squares inside a square of side 5 cm. You get 5 rows of 5, so 25 squares fit: the area is side times side. The perimeter is the walk around the edge, four equal sides. The diagonal splits the square into two right-angled triangles, and Pythagoras gives its length: d² = a² + a², so d is a times the square root of 2, about 1.414 times the side.

Worked example

Take a square with side 12 cm.

  1. Area. Area = a2. Here: 12 × 12 = 144 cm².
  2. Perimeter. Perimeter = 4a. Here: 4 × 12 = 48 cm.
  3. Diagonal. Diagonal = a√2. Here: 12 × √2 = 16.97 cm.

Finding a missing measurement

From the area, the side is its square root: an area of 144 cm² means a side of 12 cm. From the perimeter, divide by 4: a perimeter of 60 cm means a side of 15 cm, and so an area of 225 cm². From the diagonal, divide by √2.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Doubling the side does not double the area. It makes it four times as large.
  • Area is in square units (cm²) and perimeter in plain units (cm). Mixing them is the most common slip.
  • To find the side from the area, take the square root. To find it from the perimeter, divide by 4.

Where you see it

Floor tiles, chessboard squares, carrom boards, a sheet of origami paper and many window panes are squares.
