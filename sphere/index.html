Sphere

A sphere is a perfectly round solid, the shape of a ball. Every point on its surface is the same distance, the radius, from its centre.

Formulas

  • Volume = (4 ⁄ 3) πr3
  • Surface area = 4πr2

r = radius.

Open the calculator

Properties

  • One curved face, no edges and no corners.
  • Every flat slice through a sphere is a circle. A slice through the centre gives the largest one, called a great circle.
  • Of all solids with a given surface area, the sphere holds the most.

Why the formulas work

Archimedes showed that a sphere takes up exactly two thirds of the cylinder that just contains it. That cylinder has radius r and height 2r, so its volume is 2πr³, and two thirds of that is (4/3)πr³. He also showed that the surface of the sphere equals the curved surface of the same cylinder: 2πr × 2r = 4πr², exactly four times the area of a great circle.

Worked example

Take a sphere with radius 7 cm.

  1. Volume. Volume = (4 ⁄ 3) πr3. Here: (4 ⁄ 3) × π × 7 × 7 × 7 = 1,436.76 cm³ (with π = 22/7: 1,437.33 cm³).
  2. Surface area. Surface area = 4πr2. Here: 4 × π × 7 × 7 = 615.75 cm² (with π = 22/7: 616 cm²).

Finding a missing measurement

From the surface area, r = √(S ÷ 4π): 616 cm² gives r² = 49, so r = 7 cm, taking π = 22/7. From the volume, r is the cube root of 3V ÷ 4π: 38,808 cm³ gives r³ = 9261, so r = 21 cm.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Using r² in the volume or r³ in the surface area.
  • Using the diameter in place of the radius.
  • Doubling the radius makes the surface four times and the volume eight times as large.

Where you see it

Cricket balls, footballs, marbles, laddoos, ball bearings, soap bubbles and, very nearly, the Earth and the Moon are spheres.
