Types of triangles and quadrilaterals

Triangles and quadrilaterals each come in a small family of named types. Knowing the family tree matters, because a property proved for one type holds for every type inside it: whatever is true of all parallelograms is true of every rectangle, rhombus and square.

Triangles by their sides

  • Scalene: all three sides different, and so all three angles different.
  • Isosceles: two sides equal, and the two angles opposite them equal.
  • Equilateral: three sides equal, and every angle 60°.

Triangles by their angles

  • Acute-angled: all three angles less than 90°.
  • Right-angled: one angle exactly 90°.
  • Obtuse-angled: one angle more than 90°.

The two ways of sorting combine. A triangle can be right-angled and isosceles (angles 90°, 45°, 45°), but never right-angled and equilateral, since an equilateral triangle has only 60° angles. A triangle can have at most one right angle or one obtuse angle, because the three must total 180°.

The quadrilateral family

  • Trapezium: one pair of opposite sides parallel.
  • Parallelogram: both pairs of opposite sides parallel.
  • Rectangle: a parallelogram with right angles.
  • Rhombus: a parallelogram with all sides equal.
  • Square: a rectangle that is also a rhombus.
  • Kite: two pairs of equal sides next to each other.

The angles of every quadrilateral add up to 360°.

Questions that test the family tree

  • Is every square a rectangle? Yes. It has four right angles.
  • Is every rectangle a square? No. Its sides need not be equal.
  • Is every rhombus a parallelogram? Yes.
  • Is a square a rhombus? Yes. All its sides are equal.
  • Is a parallelogram a trapezium? By the definition "at least one pair of parallel sides", yes. Many Indian school books define a trapezium as having exactly one pair, and then it is not. Follow your book.

How to identify a shape from its diagonals

Diagonals that bisect each other mean a parallelogram. If they are also equal, it is a rectangle. If they are also at right angles, it is a rhombus. If both, it is a square. This test is often quicker than measuring sides and angles.
