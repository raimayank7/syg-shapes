Ellipse

An ellipse is a stretched circle, the shape of an oval. Its longest width is the major axis and its shortest is the minor axis. Half of each is called a semi-axis, written a and b.

Formulas

  • Area = πab
  • Perimeter ≈ π [3(a + b) − √((3a + b)(a + 3b))]

a = semi-major axis a and b = semi-minor axis b.

Open the calculator

Properties

  • It has two lines of symmetry, along its two axes.
  • When a equals b, the ellipse is a circle.
  • It has two special points called foci. For every point on the curve, the two distances to the foci add up to the same total, 2a.
  • The planets move round the Sun in ellipses.

Why the formulas work

An ellipse is a circle of radius b stretched sideways by the factor a/b. Stretching in one direction multiplies every area by the same factor, so the area is πb² × a/b = πab. The perimeter is harder. It has no simple exact formula, so a very accurate approximation found by the Indian mathematician Srinivasa Ramanujan is used.

Worked example

Take a ellipse with semi-major axis a 10 cm and semi-minor axis b 6 cm.

  1. Area. Area = πab. Here: π × 10 × 6 = 188.5 cm² (with π = 22/7: 188.57 cm²).
  2. Perimeter (approx.). Perimeter ≈ π [3(a + b) − √((3a + b)(a + 3b))]. Here: π × [3 × (10 + 6) − √((3 × 10 + 6) × (10 + 3 × 6))] = 51.05 cm (with π = 22/7: 51.07 cm).

Finding a missing measurement

From the area and one semi-axis, the other is A ÷ (π × the known one). An area of 132 cm² with a = 7 cm gives b = 6 cm, taking π = 22/7.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Using the full axes in place of the semi-axes. Halve them first.
  • Expecting an exact perimeter. Every simple perimeter formula for an ellipse is an approximation.

Where you see it

Running tracks seen from above (roughly), the outline of an egg (roughly), a circle seen at a slant such as the top of a glass, and the orbits of planets are ellipses.
