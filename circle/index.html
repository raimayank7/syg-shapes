Circle

A circle is the set of all points at the same distance from a centre. That distance is the radius. A line across the circle through the centre is a diameter, and it is twice the radius. The distance round the circle is the circumference.

Formulas

  • Area = πr2
  • Circumference = 2πr
  • Diameter = 2r

r = radius.

Open the calculator

Properties

  • Every diameter is a line of symmetry.
  • The circumference divided by the diameter is the same for every circle. That number is π, about 3.14159.
  • A chord is a line joining two points on the circle. The diameter is the longest chord.
  • A tangent touches the circle at one point and is at right angles to the radius there.

Why the formulas work

The circumference is π times the diameter by the very definition of π. For the area, cut the circle into many thin slices like a pizza and arrange them head to tail. They form a shape close to a rectangle, with height r and width half the circumference, πr. Its area is πr × r. The thinner the slices, the closer the fit.

Worked example

Take a circle with radius 7 cm.

  1. Area. Area = πr2. Here: π × 7 × 7 = 153.94 cm² (with π = 22/7: 154 cm²).
  2. Circumference. Circumference = 2πr. Here: 2 × π × 7 = 43.98 cm (with π = 22/7: 44 cm).
  3. Diameter. Diameter = 2r. Here: 2 × 7 = 14 cm.

Finding a missing measurement

From the circumference, r = C ÷ 2π: a circumference of 44 cm gives r = 7 cm, taking π = 22/7. From the area, r = √(A ÷ π): an area of 616 cm² gives r² = 196, so r = 14 cm.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Using the diameter in place of the radius. Halve the diameter first.
  • Mixing up 2πr (a length) and πr² (an area).
  • Squaring π as well as r: it is π × r × r.

Where you see it

Wheels, coins, rotis, bangles, clock faces and manhole covers are circles. A manhole cover is round because a circle cannot fall through its own hole.
