Semicircle

A semicircle is half of a circle, cut along a diameter. Its boundary has two parts: the curved edge and the straight diameter.

Formulas

  • Area = ½ πr2
  • Curved edge = πr
  • Perimeter = πr + 2r

r = radius.

Open the calculator

Properties

  • Its area is half the area of the full circle.
  • Its curved edge is half the circumference.
  • Any angle drawn in a semicircle, with its corner on the curve and its arms to the ends of the diameter, is a right angle.
  • It has one line of symmetry.

Why the formulas work

Half a circle has half the area: ½πr². The perimeter needs care. The curved edge is half the circumference, πr, but the shape is closed by the diameter as well, which adds 2r. So the perimeter is πr + 2r, not just πr.

Worked example

Take a semicircle with radius 7 cm.

  1. Area. Area = ½ πr2. Here: ½ × π × 7 × 7 = 76.97 cm² (with π = 22/7: 77 cm²).
  2. Curved edge. Curved edge = πr. Here: π × 7 = 21.99 cm (with π = 22/7: 22 cm).
  3. Perimeter. Perimeter = πr + 2r. Here: π × 7 + 2 × 7 = 35.99 cm (with π = 22/7: 36 cm).

Finding a missing measurement

From the perimeter, r = P ÷ (π + 2). With π = 22/7 that is P × 7 ÷ 36, so a perimeter of 72 cm gives r = 14 cm. From the area, r = √(2A ÷ π): an area of 77 cm² gives r² = 49, so r = 7 cm.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Leaving out the diameter when asked for the perimeter.
  • Halving the radius. Only the area and the curve are halved, not r.

Where you see it

A protractor, a hand fan opened fully, arches over doors and windows, and a slice of watermelon are semicircles.
