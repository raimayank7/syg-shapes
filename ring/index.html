Ring (annulus)

A ring, or annulus, is the region between two circles that share a centre. Questions about a path round a circular park or pond are ring questions.

Formulas

  • Area = π(R2 − r2)
  • Outer edge + inner edge = 2π(R + r)
  • Width = R − r

R = outer radius and r = inner radius.

Open the calculator

Properties

  • Its width is the difference of the two radii.
  • Its area is the difference of the areas of the two circles.
  • It has two edges: the outer circle and the inner circle.

Why the formulas work

Take the big circle and remove the small one from its middle. What is left has area πR² − πr², which is tidied to π(R² − r²). A useful form is π(R + r)(R − r): the width of the ring times the length of the circle running along its middle.

Worked example

Take a ring (annulus) with outer radius 14 cm and inner radius 7 cm.

  1. Area. Area = π(R2 − r2). Here: π × (14² − 7²) = 461.81 cm² (with π = 22/7: 462 cm²).
  2. Total edge. Outer edge + inner edge = 2π(R + r). Here: 2 × π × (14 + 7) = 131.95 cm (with π = 22/7: 132 cm).
  3. Width. Width = R − r. Here: 14 − 7 = 7 cm.

Finding a missing measurement

If the area and the inner radius are known, R² = r² + A ÷ π. An inner radius of 7 cm and an area of 462 cm² give R² = 49 + 147 = 196, so R = 14 cm and the ring is 7 cm wide.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Subtracting the radii first and then squaring: π(R − r)² is wrong.
  • For a path of width w outside a circle of radius r, the outer radius is r + w, not w.

Where you see it

A circular path round a fountain, a washer, a bangle laid flat, a CD and the cross-section of a pipe are rings.
