All mensuration formulas

Area and perimeter of flat shapes, surface area and volume of solids, on one page. Press Print for a clean formula sheet.

How to use this sheet

The formulas are grouped into flat shapes and solids. In every formula a letter stands for a length: a, b and c for sides, l for a length or a slant height, h for a height measured at right angles, r and R for radii, and p and q for diagonals. All lengths must be in the same unit. Areas then come out in square units and volumes in cubic units. Tap a shape's name for its figure, the reason behind each formula and worked examples.

The sheet covers 16 flat shapes and 10 solids. Where a shape has more than one useful measure, such as the curved and the total surface of a cylinder, each is listed on its own line. Square roots are written with the √ sign, and π stands for the usual constant, about 3.1416 or 22/7.

A formula sheet is for revision, not for learning. Formulas stay in the mind when you know where they come from, so read the "Why the formulas work" part of a shape once before you rely on the sheet.

Patterns worth noticing

  • Half of something. A triangle is half a parallelogram, and a rhombus or kite is half the rectangle drawn round its diagonals.
  • Base area times height. Cube, cuboid, prism and cylinder all use it for volume.
  • One third. A cone is a third of its cylinder and a pyramid a third of its prism.
  • Powers match dimensions. A length formula has lengths to the first power, an area formula has two lengths multiplied, and a volume formula three. If your formula for an area has only one length in it, something is missing.
