Cube

A cube is a solid with six equal square faces. All its edges are equal. Volume itself is defined by it: one cubic centimetre is the space filled by a cube of edge 1 cm.

Formulas

  • Volume = a3
  • Total surface area = 6a2
  • Lateral surface area = 4a2
  • Diagonal = a√3

a = edge.

Open the calculator

Properties

  • 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 corners.
  • Every face is a square and every angle is a right angle.
  • The lateral surface is the four side faces, leaving out the top and the bottom.
  • The space diagonal runs from one corner through the centre to the opposite corner.

Why the formulas work

A cube of edge 4 cm holds 4 layers, each of 4 rows of 4 unit cubes: 4 × 4 × 4 = 64. So the volume is the edge cubed. Each of the six faces is a square of area a², giving 6a² for the whole surface. The space diagonal uses Pythagoras twice: first across a face (a√2), then up through the cube, giving a√3.

Worked example

Take a cube with edge 5 cm.

  1. Volume. Volume = a3. Here: 5 × 5 × 5 = 125 cm³.
  2. Total surface area. Total surface area = 6a2. Here: 6 × 5 × 5 = 150 cm².
  3. Lateral surface area. Lateral surface area = 4a2. Here: 4 × 5 × 5 = 100 cm².
  4. Space diagonal. Diagonal = a√3. Here: 5 × √3 = 8.66 cm.

Finding a missing measurement

From the volume, the edge is the cube root: 343 cm³ gives an edge of 7 cm. From the total surface area, a = √(T ÷ 6): 150 cm² gives a² = 25, so a = 5 cm and the volume is 125 cm³.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Doubling the edge makes the volume eight times as large, not double.
  • Giving volume in square units. Volume is in cubic units (cm³).
  • Using 4a² when the question asks for the total surface.

Where you see it

Dice, sugar cubes, ice cubes, a puzzle cube and some packing boxes are cubes.
